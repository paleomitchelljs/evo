One sentence to carry forward

The difference between a significant result and a non-significant result is itself not significant. If you run two tests and one comes back clearly different and the other does not, that doesn't mean one effect is bigger than the other. It means you're sure about one and not sure about the other. Due to sample size, you can have a situation where the more effective drug — the bigger underlying effect — has higher uncertainty and so isn't significant, and your best guess is that it's actually better than the significant one.

The empirical P answers exactly one question: is the observed δ̂ outside the cloud of shuffled δ̂'s, given this n, this σ, and this regression model? Change any of those three inputs — sample size, within-group variation, or which statistic you fit — and the verdict can move while the underlying biology stays put. A P-value is a property of the test, not of the truth.
