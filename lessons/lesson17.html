Lesson 17 — Helping relatives when cooperation can spread

BIO 202, Spring 2026, draft v1. Hamilton's rule: r·b > c. The first lesson where the right unit of selection isn't always the individual.

What you'll do

Set up a selfish population. Add an altruism allele. Find the r·b = c boundary. Apply to haplodiploid bees, where r = 0.75 between sisters predicts worker sterility.

Hamilton's rule. Kin selection happens when r×b > c. r is relatedness. b is the benefit to the recipient. c is the cost to the giver. A big benefit to a distant relative can be okay. A small benefit to a close relative can be okay. Random stranger needs a kidney — most won't give. Family member — many will.— 461_lec22_05

C — The r·b = c boundary

Map the (b, c) phase plane at fixed r. Above the r·b = c line, altruism spreads. Below, it doesn't. Move the r slider; watch the line tilt.

Complete Stage B.

Scenario

The line r·b = c divides the (b, c) plane into two regions. With r = 0.5, the line is c = 0.5·b: altruism spreads only when the benefit is at least twice the cost. With r = 1 (clones), any net benefit spreads. With r = 0 (strangers), nothing spreads.

D — Bees — worker sterility from haplodiploid math

Female bees are diploid; males are haploid. Sisters share 75% (50% from mother recombined, 100% from haploid father). Workers helping their queen produce more sisters wins over reproducing themselves.

Complete Stage C.

Scenario

For a hymenopteran female, the relatedness to:

• own daughter = 0.5 (standard diploid offspring inheritance)
• full sister (same mother and haploid father) = 0.75
• brother = 0.25 (gets only mother's contribution)

A worker maximizes her gene's spread by helping her mother make more sisters, not by reproducing herself. Without any altruism allele needing to be "selected for," the math predicts worker sterility from first principles.

One sentence to carry forward

r·b > c is the rule that takes altruism and turns it into selfish help. You are not helping your brother. You are helping your genes inside your brother. The math of inclusive fitness predicts where cooperation will spread — and in haplodiploid bees, it predicts worker sterility from first principles.— composite: 202_lec32_04 + 461_lec29_01
