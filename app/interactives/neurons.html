threshold A neuron fires when the number of its excitatory inputs that are firing, minus the number of its inhibitory inputs that are firing, reaches this number. Set to 0, it fires with no input at all, until something holds it down. 1 step 0 Time runs in steps. At each step every neuron fires or stays silent according to what reached it: sensors as they are now, other neurons as they were one step ago. A signal takes one step to cross from one neuron to the next. Silence wipes every neuron back to silent, as if the animal had just woken.
Sensors on the left fire while their stimulus is there. Click one to switch it on or off. Muscles on the right contract while the one neuron wired into them fires. Drag from the dot on the right edge of a sensor or neuron onto a neuron or a muscle to connect them. Drop it back on the same neuron and it feeds itself. A new connection excites (arrow); click it once to make it inhibit (bar), and again to remove it. Click a neuron to set its threshold or remove it; drag it to move it. Every cell is either firing (filled) or silent (open). Each wire carries its signal one way, out of the dot and into the next cell, but wires may loop back to earlier cells.

Every combination One row for each way the stimuli can be on (●) or off (○), each held steady from a silent start until the circuit settles. For each muscle, the left column is what the animal should do; the right is what your wiring does. ~ means the muscle never settles: it keeps switching. Click a row to set the sensors to it.

The trial The stimuli switch on and off over the steps shown, from left to right, starting from silence. Pale rows are what each muscle should do; dark rows are what your wiring does. A muscle may answer a step or more late, as long as every muscle is late by the same amount the whole way through. Play it to watch it run on the board.
