You are the bird on the left. One suitor arrives each day of the breeding season, and you either pair with it or let it go. It does not come back. The bar under a suitor is its display, and it is honest: a display of 80 means each chick from that pairing has an 80% chance to fledge. The row along the bottom is where your young can go. Each egg takes the colour of the bird it came from.

Every rule, two thousand seasons Across: the lowest display a bird will pair with. All the way left, it pairs with everyone; to the right, it holds out for better. Up: young fledged per season by a bird that plays that rule every time, averaged over 2000 seasons of random suitors, as a multiple of what pairing with everyone gives. Above ×1, holding out pays; below it, it costs. The solid line is the sex you are playing; the dashed line is the other sex in the same species. The dotted line is you so far, measured the same way against the suitors you actually met.
