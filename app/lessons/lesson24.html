  • One cell from the tail of a mouse embryo and one of its genes, Hes7. The gene is copied into mRNA; the mRNA is ready only after its introns are cut out; each mRNA is read into Hes7 protein; both are broken down. Hes7 protein can bind its own gene and shut it off.

The cellThe nucleus holds the Hes7 gene (its three introns drawn as gaps; an intron removed is gone from the drawing). Each small stroke is a Hes7 mRNA; each dot is 25 molecules of Hes7 protein. The cell every 20 minutesThe same cell, one small picture every 20 minutes from minute 0 (top left) to minute 700 (bottom right): the nucleus shaded by how much Hes7 protein there is (light little, dark a lot), the strokes its mRNA.

Gene, mRNA, proteinTranscription makes mRNA at a fixed rate when nothing shuts the gene; the mRNA is ready after the delay; translation makes protein from it (grey arrows: fixed). Each arrow to broken down is a half-life: how long until half the molecules made are gone.The red arrow back to the gene: the protein shutting its own gene off (dashed: it cannot bind).
