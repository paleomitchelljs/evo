A heterozygote is an individual with two different genetic ancestors for that locus.

  • Four hundred individuals, one locus, two alleles. Each individual is two halves, one per copy.
  • Nothing dies and nothing is selected. The only control is who pairs with whom.
  • F: how far the heterozygote count falls short of what random pairing would give.

R code

# 400 individuals, two copies each, at one locusp <- 0.5          # how common the dark allele isf <- 0            # how often a partner is a relativeHo <- mean(a != b)   # heterozygotes, countedp  <- mean(c(a, b))   # the frequency they implyHe <- 2 * p * (1 - p)  # what random pairing would giveF  <- 1 - Ho / He

Moose again. You fitted this herd with a birth rate and a death rate in Lesson 6. Now a gene lives in it.

  • Top plot: the herd, 120 years of it, built from your birth and death rates. Bad years are marked.
  • Bottom plot: F — how much of the founders' heterozygotes is gone.
  • Nothing is selected. Only the coin and the headcount.

You inherit alleles, not genotypes. A genotype is a pairing, made fresh every generation and taken apart again when that individual breeds.

  • The knob rebuilds the tree with more or fewer matings between relatives.
  • Bottom plot: how common each founder allele is in each generation, and F in each generation. Faint lines are single drops.
