A

Locked — confirm your name above to begin.
  • A season on the island: garter snakes hunt salamanders, and hawks hunt snakes. You are the snake outlined in red; steer with the mouse. A season opens over the page; at winter you come back to the pedigree.
  • The hawks come early in the season and take every snake above ten: a hawk's shadow falls where its snake is heading, so get out of it. Then 30 salamanders come out over the season, and a young snake costs 2. A snake that eats nothing dies before winter. At winter the fed snakes pair at random, yours too; each pair has as many young as their salamanders pay for.
  • Next season you play one of your young, up to generation 15. If your snake dies, or is left without a mate, your game is over, and the seasons left to generation 15 play themselves.
  • Every snake carries one chromosome from each parent, with seven genes on it: loci 1 to 7. Every snake in the first generation carries one new allele, a colour; the rest start plain grey. After that, one new allele turns up each generation, in a young snake at random. None of them does anything.
  • Pedigree, below: one row a generation, and only the snakes that had young. Three views: each snake as a snake; as its two chromosomes (mother's above, father's below); or as its two copies of one locus. A ring marks the copy where each allele arose.
  • Click a snake, or a copy, for lines to where it came from (red, up) and where it went (blue, down). Tick lock to keep those lines while you click another.

B

Finish Stage A to unlock this section.
  • A new island, the same game, the same pedigree. Play to generation 15.
  • This time every new allele does something. Each comes with an s and an h, drawn at random from the curves below. A snake with two copies of it has its salamanders count × (1 − s); with one copy, × (1 − h·s). Across its seven genes the numbers multiply.
  • s = −0.5 with two copies: 2 salamanders count as 3, and pay for one and a half young. s = 0.5: they count as 1.
  • Click a copy in the pedigree to see its allele's s and h.

New alleles: where s and h come from

C

Finish Stage B to unlock this section.
  • Six snakes from an island like yours, paired three ways, and one gene: each snake's two copies, gold for one allele and grey for the rest. Below each pair, their young; a line runs from each copy a young carries up to the parent copy it was copied from.
  • Click + or − over a pair for more or fewer young. Click a young's copy to take it from the parent's other copy instead. Click the small diamond under a copy to have it come out changed: gold to grey, grey to gold.
  • Under the snakes, the change in the allele's share, split as in Lesson 13: who had more young, cov(w, z), and whether their young carry what they carried, E(wΔz). Under E(wΔz), the same split one level down, among the two copies inside each snake.
  • Each target: the young's share of the gold allele inside the band, keeping to the rules listed. Tick practice for a run that does not count.
