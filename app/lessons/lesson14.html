  • One population, one generation at a time. Each individual has, on average, the births per individual number of offspring, and dies with the deaths per individual chance.
  • Crowding is how many there are. Its two arrows are how much each extra hundred individuals changes births and deaths: blue raises, red lowers.
  • The other factors arrows are births and deaths when there is no crowding at all.
  • Top plot: births and deaths per individual against how many there are. Where births are higher the population grows; where deaths are higher it shrinks.
  • Bottom plot: the population over 40 generations, from 20 individuals. Each round, one arrow is yours; the rest are held.

  • Stage A's two lines, reduced to one: births minus deaths per individual. Where it meets the left edge is how fast the population grows when uncrowded (r); where it falls to zero is the crowding that stops growth (K).
  • Some individuals carry a trait, and pass it to all their offspring. Its two arrows move r and K for those that carry it: blue raises, red lowers, and you set each one.
  • Everyone shares one crowding: carriers and non-carriers count toward it alike.
  • Each run starts with 50 individuals, half of them carriers, and runs 100 generations.
  • Top plot: births minus deaths for carriers and for the rest. Middle: the share carrying the trait. Bottom: how many of each.
