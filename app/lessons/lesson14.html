Humans like patterns. So when you think of "random," you want it to be: a 1, then a 6, then a 2, then a 5, then a 3, then a 4. That's not how it works. It's very streaky — you'll get three or four 6s in a row.

Streakiness begs for an explanation in our brains, and you have to resist that tendency. Streakiness does not always beg for an explanation.

FGFR3 is the receptor for fibroblast growth factor — the protein that tells limb cells to grow. In dachshunds, we bred them to have short legs — under positive selection.

        
If you look at dachshund DNA versus non-dachshund DNA around FGFR3, dachshunds have basically zero genetic diversity in a region of about 20 megabases on either side. That's a really big region of zero diversity — compared to normal dogs where limb length has been under pretty good selection but diversity is way higher than zero.
