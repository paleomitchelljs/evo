A

Locked — confirm your name above to begin.
  • 144 letters of a bacterium's DNA, read 5′ to 3′. Above them, the amino acids they code for: from the first ATG (the start) to the first stop in step with it, three letters at a time.
  • The wheel is the genetic code. Read a codon from the middle out: its first letter, its second, its third, then the amino acid. Point at a codon in the sequence to light it on the wheel.
  • Click a letter to change it to one of the other three, to put a new letter in front of it, or to take it out. The amino acids are read again after every change, from wherever the start and the stop now fall.
  • Each target asks for one kind of change. Each change starts from the sequence as it was.
  • If the protein changed, set what it does: one copy of the changed gene multiplies an individual's young by 1 − h·s, two copies by 1 − s. If not, s = 0.
  • Go puts your change into one copy of the gene in each of 1,000 populations of N individuals, and runs each one for 4N generations. Each square is one of the first 100. Set N on your first change: it holds for all eight.
  • Tick practice for a run that does not count.
  • After all eight, your changes side by side.

B

  
Finish Stage A to unlock this section.


  

    
    
    
      
  • Each individual: two chromosomes, ten genes on each. A red gene carries a harmful change. Each harmful change multiplies an individual's young by 1 − s; here s = 0.1, so three of them leave 0.9 × 0.9 × 0.9 = 0.73 as many young.
    • 
      
  • Without sex, a young is a copy of its one parent, both chromosomes whole. Press Copy.
    • 
      
  • With sex, each parent makes four gametes: click a gap between two genes to put that parent's crossover there, then Make gametes. Click one gamete from each parent and the two fuse into a young.
    • 
      
  • Twenty young each: the parent copied twenty times, and twenty young of the parent and its mate, crossovers at random. Each copy carries all of its parent; each young from sex carries half of each parent.
    • 
      
  • Then whole populations, one without sex and one with: every young also picks up new harmful changes, U of them on average, and none is ever undone. The plot follows the best individual in each: the fewest harmful changes anyone carries.
    • 
      
  • Each target: set the slider that is open, press Go, and both populations run for 200 generations. Tick practice for a run that does not count.
    • 
      
  • Then one population, two ways to breed. A trait set by eight genes on each chromosome; the closer an individual is to the best value, the more young it has. Most females mate; a few copy themselves, and so do their daughters. A female makes the same number of young either way, so each of an asexual female's young carries all of her, and each of a sexual female's carries half. Set how often the best value moves, and how far.

      • Watch first. Pick an event from the menu and press Play: the chromosomes move into the pairing that makes it, cross over once, and come apart into what it makes. Watch each one; then make them yourself.

      • One individual's two chromosomes before meiosis: eight genes, A to H, each with an arrow for the way it reads. The grey arrows between some genes are copies of one jumping gene (Lesson 15): the same stretch of DNA, facing one way or the other. The black dot is the centre the chromosome is pulled by when the cell divides.
        • 
      
      • Drag the lower chromosome along the upper one. It snaps where the genes line up with their partners, or where a grey arrow sits over a grey arrow.

        • 
      
      • With the genes lined up, click a gap to put the crossover there. Paired at grey arrows, the crossover falls in them. Then Cross over: the chromosomes break where they touch and join the other way.

        D
        
  
        Finish Stage C to unlock this section.
        

  
        
    
          
      
        • Fifty individuals, each with two chromosomes of twelve genes. Three of them carry one copy of a new chromosome: its alleles are blue. On it, genes 4 to 8 are the other way round: an inversion (the red bracket).
          • 
      
        • The new chromosome's allele at gene 6 helps: one copy multiplies an individual's young by 1 − s/2, two by 1 − s (s below 0 helps; 0 is drift alone).
          • 
      
        • An individual with one inverted chromosome and one standard crosses over outside genes 4 to 8 as usual; a crossover inside them makes no working gamete (the last event in C). Two inverted chromosomes cross over anywhere.
          • 
      
        • Run plays the population generation by generation until the helpful allele is in every chromosome or in none. Then untick the inversion and run it again.
