Survival, mating, offspringA new allele from the orange pond and one from the blue, at different loci, form an incompatible pair with chance 0.05 (Lesson 19 C: 0.02). One copy of each in an offspring is enough. Until the floods they cannot meet, since each arose in a different pond.Red arrow: how much each incompatible pair lowers survival, held fixed during the targets; once they are done, click and drag it.The choosy allele: a fish with two copies rejects a partner with the chance on the "how choosy" arrow, scaled by how unlike their mixes of new alleles are; with one copy, half that. After 4 rejections it mates with the next fish it meets. When the floods begin, 1 chromosome in 10 in each pond carries it.

From wing length to speciation and extinctionA bird's wing length is its genetic value plus an environmental effect. Within a species, wing length varies by the additive genetic variance (VA) and the environmental variance (VE): the arrows into wing length; thicker, larger. In the wing-length box: dark, the birds' genetic values; light, their wing lengths. Drag the curve sideways to set the first species' average.Gene flow: the chance a bird disperses to another population of its species. Range size: the share of the largest possible range that a species with such wings holds, which sets its census size. Each depends on wing length: drag either end of a line. Dots: clade 1's living species.Gene flow holds a species' populations together, leaving fewer fragments to become new species. A larger range holds more birds, so the species is less likely to go extinct. Click an arrow, then drag it.At each speciation event, a fragment cut off from the rest becomes the new species. It starts from one bird that did not disperse, whose genetic value becomes the new species' average. So a new species differs from its parent by about the square root of VA in mm (VA is in mm²), and leans toward the wing lengths of birds that stay.
