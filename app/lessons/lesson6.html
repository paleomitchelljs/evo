C

Solve Stage B to unlock this section.

A birth rate that never changes is a claim about the island: that 1983, when 1,359 mm of rain fell, was no different for a nesting cactus finch than 1985, when four millimetres fell.

So let the rate change. Draw an arrow from the rain to the birth rate, and a second number appears: how much the year's rain moves it. Draw an arrow from the birds themselves to the death rate and you get the other kind of cause — the one that comes from inside the population.

Click an arrow to add it or take it away. The numbers it brings appear and disappear with it.

  • The same 37 counts, the same start, the same run.
  • Rain is the island's total for that year, from 1 mm to 1,359 mm.
  • A drawn arrow adds one number to the model. An arrow you have not drawn costs nothing and does nothing.
  • The bar is a typical miss of 1.32.

Solve Stage C to unlock this section.

Moose have been counted on Isle Royale every winter since 1959. Here are the first forty-two years of it, and nothing else — no weather, no browse, no predators.

One birth rate and one death rate for the whole record, as before. Then a third number: how much extra of the herd dies in a bad year. You decide which years were bad by clicking them.

Every year you mark costs you a click and buys you some fit. Find the fewest that get you there.

  • 42 winter counts, 1959 to 2000. The model starts on the 1959 count.
  • Click any year on the strip below the plot to mark it as a bad year, and click again to unmark it.
  • Every marked year gets the same extra death rate — you are choosing which years, not how bad each one was.
