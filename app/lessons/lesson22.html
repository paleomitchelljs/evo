A

Locked — confirm your name above to begin.
  • An island of nests, one bird to a nest. A bird with the helping allele helps its four neighbours raise more young, and raises fewer itself. Young settle near where they hatched. See when helping spreads.
  • Benefit, the blue arrow from the neighbours' helping; cost, the red arrow from helping; how close young settle, the arrow between a bird's helping gene and its neighbours'.

The islandThe island: 32 x 32 nests (the edges wrap round). Each square a nest; its shade, the bird's helping alleles: pale none, middle one, dark two; blank, a nest no young bird took this generation.Run 1, at the generation the plots have reached.

Genes, helping, youngHelping gene: two alleles, a helper and a non-helper; a bird with one helps half as much.Cost, the red arrow from helping: how many fewer young a full helper has (of about 2). Benefit, the blue arrow from the neighbours' helping: how many more young a bird has when all four of its neighbours are full helpers (a quarter for each).How close young settle, the arrow between a bird's helping gene and its neighbours': the young take a nest within that many nests of where they hatched, so the closer they settle, the more a bird's neighbours share its parents, and its alleles. It sets who the neighbours are, not what helping does.Grey arrows: fixed.

Target1,024 nests; at the start every nest full, half the helping alleles helpers (in C, all). Each bird's young take nests near where they hatched; a young bird whose nest is taken looks for an empty one around it; young with none left die. Go plays 100 generations (in C, 150), 16 times.The picture: what a hit looks like; the red box, where the thick line, the 16 runs' average, should end.
