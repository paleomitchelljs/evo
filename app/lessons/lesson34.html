C — Why we exist at the level we do

Your neurons cooperate because they're nearly genetically identical. The cost to a neuron to help another nearly identical neuron isn't actually a cost — genetically speaking. That's why thought is possible — your neurons aren't trying to destroy each other. — 202_lec30_03

D — The story of George Price

George Price hated his own equation. He wanted altruism to be real. So he set out to live a refutation of it. He converted to Christianity, gave away all his stuff, and went around doing random acts of kindness that in no way benefited him — for strangers in another country he had no relatedness to. — 202_lec32_03

Nothing to compute here. Price wrote down the identity you have been running all lesson, saw what it said about why anything helps anything else, and could not live with it. He died in 1975, by his own hand, in a squat in London. The equation is not wrong. Sit with that for a moment before the last panel.

E — Course bookend

Evolution is not like gravity. It's not a force that exists independent of things. It's more like motion. Evolution is the net change that results from many forces — a lot of things operating at once causing a change. That net change is what we call evolution. — 202_lec01_03

A net change, coming apart into named pieces. You have been doing that since you first put a line through a cloud of dots. The last thing to add is that the things those forces act on — the gene, the chromosome, the cell, you, the colony, the lineage — are not given in advance. Each one became a thing to act on when the accounting closed around it.
