A

Locked — confirm your name above to begin.

A hundred individuals, a trait that varies between them, and sixty generations. Half of what makes an individual big or small is inherited by its offspring; the other half is not.

One knob to start with: how strongly the trait goes with the number of offspring an individual leaves. Push it up and the big ones out-breed the small ones. Push it below zero and it is the small ones.

Then set it to nothing at all — no advantage either way — and run it again.

  • The solid line is the inherited part of the trait. The pale dots are what you would have measured that year.
  • Ended — how far the line finished from where it began.
  • Strayed — the furthest it ever got from where it began, at any point.
  • Both are in units of the spread the population started with.
  • A batch is ten populations. The test is judged on the worst of the ten, because one flat run proves nothing.

B

Solve Stage A to unlock this section.

With no advantage at all the population still went somewhere, and it went there because a hundred is not very many. Every generation, which individuals happen to breed is a draw, and a draw off a hundred is a coarse one.

So take more of them. The slider goes from twenty-five up to eight hundred. Work up the ladder with the advantage held at zero, and watch what the two numbers do.

You have seen this exchange rate before, in a completely different place.

  • Both knobs stay live. The ladder only means something with the advantage at zero.
  • Every rung is kept in the panel below. It records the middle of all the runs you have done at that size, which is steadier to read a pattern off than the worst of them — and it steadies further the more you run.
  • Eight hundred is where the slider stops. It is not where the change stops.

C

Solve Stage B to unlock this section.

Half of what made an individual big or small has been inherited this whole time. That was a setting, and here it becomes a knob.

The panel underneath shows one generation of parents against their offspring. Turn the knob up and the cloud tightens into a line. Turn it down and it goes round — offspring that look nothing like the parents they came from.

So turn it down. Take it as low as the slider goes, with as many individuals as the slider allows and no advantage at all, and see whether that is enough.

  • The knob stops at 0.20. It does not go to nothing here.
  • Lower panel: each dot is one parent's departure from the crowd, against their offspring's.
  • All three knobs stay live.

D

Solve Stage C to unlock this section.

Two things change here. The inherited knob now goes all the way to nothing, and a fourth knob appears: how different from each other the individuals are in the first place.

Either one of them will do it. Take the inherited fraction to zero and the line goes dead flat — the dots still bounce, because you are still measuring a hundred individuals, but nothing carries from one generation to the next. Take the differences to zero and there is nothing to bounce.

Do it both ways. Then look at what you had to switch off to get there.

  • The test: both numbers at or under 0.05, on the worst of ten.
  • Passing it once by each route opens the last part.
  • The pale dots are what you would have measured. Watch what they do when the line goes flat.
