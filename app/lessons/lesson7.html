A

Locked — confirm your name above to begin.

The way people talk about evolution, usually, is that it's like a force — like gravity. This is wrong. It's more like motion. Evolution is the net change that results from many forces, a lot of things operating at once causing a change.

Here is an island, and twenty generations of it. It rained, seeds grew, birds ate them, and the birds that were left had chicks. Nothing else happened.

Over those twenty generations the average beak got deeper. Build a model that says why, and get it to sit on the record.

  • Forty-five generations in all. You are shown the first twenty.
  • Two kinds of seed. Soft ones, which any bird can eat. Hard ones, which take some cracking.
  • Rain makes both kinds. Birds eat both kinds. What is left at the end of a generation is what the next one starts with.
  • Every bird is followed one at a time: it eats what it can reach, and then it lives or it doesn't.
  • Below the graph is your model. Click a dashed arrow to add it, and the slider it pays for switches on.
  • Typical miss: how far your model's average beak sits from the record in an average generation, in millimetres.

D

Solve Stage C to unlock this section.

Nothing on this island was pushing in a direction. Rain fell, seeds grew, birds ate what they could reach, and some of them had chicks.

The beaks got deeper for twenty generations and then went back. A bird that arrived in generation 31 turned them round without touching a single one of your numbers.

"Evolution occurred" is a terrible explanation for an organism's trait. Read the table: the models are not separated by how many numbers they carry.

  • All three were fitted on the same twenty generations.
  • Green marks the smallest miss in each column.
  • Nothing in any of them is called selection, and nothing is called drift.
