Locked — confirm your name above to begin.
  • Two islands of finches, long apart, now with birds crossing between them. Three genes: one for beak size, one for colour, one for the colour a female likes. See what keeps the two islands' birds apart: their seeds, the females' preferences, or both.
  • Click the seeds in the Target card to set how many of each island's seeds are hard. Small beaks crack soft seeds, large beaks hard ones.
  • Preference strength, the arrow from preference on the diagram, sets how much a female favours a male of the colour she likes.

Genes, traits, reproductionThree genes, each with two alleles, on different chromosomes: beak (small, large), colour (dull, bright), preference (likes dull, likes bright). A bird with one allele of each is in between. The colour shows in males.Seeds: each island's, set in the Target card. A beak suited to them lives more.Mating: a female meets up to 4 males in turn; the mating surface shows the probability that a meeting ends in mating. If none of the 4 does, she does not breed this year.Preference strength, the arrow from preference onto colour → mating: how fast the mating probability falls as his colour moves away from the one she likes. Colour → mating follows it: dashed at 0, where his colour makes no difference to mating. Grey arrows: fixed.

The chromosome40 loci. The beak gene ● sits at locus 20 and stays there. In this stage the colour gene ■ is on the same chromosome: drag it to any other locus. The farther apart the two, the more often a crossover falls between them when an egg or sperm is made (about one crossover a chromosome). The preference gene is on another chromosome.Ticked, an inversion runs from the beak gene to the colour gene on every chromosome that starts on the blue island: in a bird with one inverted and one standard chromosome, no crossover falls inside it.
