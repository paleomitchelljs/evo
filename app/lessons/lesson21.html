Locked — confirm your name above to begin.
  • Two islands of finches, long apart, now with birds crossing between them. Three genes: one for beak size, one for colour, one for the colour a female likes. See what keeps the two islands' birds apart: their seeds, the females' preferences, or both.
  • Click the seeds in the Target card to set how many of each island's seeds are hard. Small beaks crack soft seeds, large beaks hard ones.
  • Preference strength, the arrow on the diagram, sets how much a female favours a male of the colour she likes.

Genes, traits, reproductionThree genes, each with two alleles, on different chromosomes: beak (small, large), colour (pale, dark), preference (likes pale, likes dark). A bird with one allele of each is in between.Seeds: each island's, set in the Target card. A beak suited to them lives more.Mating: a female meets up to 4 males and takes each with the chance the mating surface shows; turned down all 4, she does not breed this year.Preference strength, the arrow: how much less likely she is to take a male the further his colour is from the one she likes. Grey arrows: fixed.
