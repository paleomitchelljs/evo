Locked — confirm your name above to begin.
  • Two populations of 25 hermaphrodite genomes, side by side: orange on the left, blue on the right. Each genome: two chromosomes of 40 genes, drawn as beads.
  • A bead's hue is the population its allele arose in; its shade, which allele it is. At the start every orange genome is all orange, every blue one all blue.
  • Each generation every genome is replaced by a young from two parents in its own population, each chromosome crossed over on the way. Then each genome swaps places with one in the other population with chance m.
  • Nothing helps or harms. Go plays 50 generations in 50 pairs of populations (100 populations). Pair 1 is drawn up close.
  • A fragment: an unbroken run of the other population's hue on one chromosome.
  • Tick practice for a run that does not count.

      • One population split in two. Every allele at the start is grey: it arose before the split, and both halves carry the same ones at the same shares.

      • A new mutation takes the hue of the population it arises in: orange or blue. Set N (genomes in each population), m, and new mutations a chromosome each generation.
        • 
      
      • Heterozygosity: the chance two copies of a gene, drawn from one population, are different alleles. Within: each population on its own. Pooled: the two together.
        • 
      
      • FST: the gap between pooled and within, as a share of pooled.
        • 
      
      • Go plays 20 pairs of populations (40 populations). Under pair 1, each gene's alleles in the orange population (left bar) and the blue (right bar).
