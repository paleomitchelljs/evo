One equation for the whole thing. It adds nothing new — it names the quantity you have been computing since Lesson 3.

Selection is the correlation. Drift is the scatter around it. There's the deterministic part — the amount to which the trait covaries with fitness, with differential reproduction. And there's the random error. Because no matter how much your trait covaries with fitness, there's going to be random stuff where things get struck by lightning. You might have a gene that makes your heart great. That doesn't help you if a tiger ate you. — 202_lec11_01

B — Two levels: between and within

Assemble individuals into groups. Now there are two covariances at once.

Δz̄  =  cov(Wk, Zk)  +  Ek[ cov(wi, zi | k) ]
            between groups        within groups
Selection operates at the species level, the individual level, the cellular level, the genomic level — all manifesting at the same time. The unit of selection depends on the scope.— 202_lec29_03

C — The diagnostic: which level is selection at?

The ratio of the between-group covariance to the within-group covariance is the diagnostic. When it crosses 1, the group becomes the unit of selection.

unit = "group"  ⇔  cov(Wk, Zk) / Ek[ cov(wi, zi | k) ]  >  1
Fitness is not the number of children you have. It's the number of copies of yourself in the next generation. Self is your genes, not just your body.— 461_lec28_04
Preview. The test is the Lesson 5 shuffle, one level up: permute group membership and see how often the between-group covariance beats the within-group covariance by chance. The tail fraction is the P-value for "selection is acting at the higher level." Same permutation null, different scope.

D — The same diagnostic, three times down the stack

Nothing new is introduced here. Stage C's ratio is run at three places where a lower unit and a higher one are in competition, and the biology is different every time.

This is not cells competing with cells. This is loci in your genome competing with other loci in your genome — because the loci copy themselves, the loci have differential reproduction, and they have heritable variation.— 202_lec15_04
Preview. One panel, a three-way toggle, and the same stacked bar as Stage C. Each setting loads a different pair of levels and a different real anchor; the diagnostic and the plot never change.

Gene inside a chromosome. Two loci with a recombination dial. Wide open, selection at one locus leaves the other alone. Closed down, a sweep at one drags its neighbours and the block starts behaving as one thing. Anchor: the roughly 20-megabase stretch of near-zero diversity flanking FGFR3 in dachshunds, and the width of that stretch as a reading of how hard selection had to outrun recombination.

Gene inside a genome. Fair meiosis first, then an allele that biases itself into more than half the gametes. It wins at its own level while the genome carrying it does worse. Add a suppressor at another locus and watch which level the ratio lands on. Anchor: the Alu insertion in a Hox regulator that removed the ape tail — an element copying itself, with the tail as collateral.

Genome inside a cell. Organelles with their own replication, so there is now variation inside a single cell. The cell's counter-move is to move the essential genes into the nucleus, and the within-cell term collapses. Anchor: the failure mode — a somatic lineage that reproduces faster than its neighbours inside one body.

These were drafted as Lessons 27, 28 and 29. They were three lessons; they are three readings of one instrument, and they belong in one stage.

E — Re-derive Hamilton's rule

Swap the fitness function for the altruism form w(z) = 1 − c·z + b·z̄k and recompute both Price terms.

altruism favored  ⇔  b · r  >  c
