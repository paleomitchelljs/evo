q <- read.csv("data/clean/lotr_quotes.csv")pace <- function(d) coef(lm(page ~ minute, data = d))[2]cloud <- sapply(split(q, q$film), function(d)  replicate(2000, pace(d[sample(nrow(d), nrow(d), replace = TRUE), ])))apply(cloud, 2, quantile, c(.025, .975))

Showcase — how old the bottom of a cliff has to be

The White Cliffs of Dover are about 100 m of chalk: the shells of single-celled algae, roughly 250 to the millimetre, laid down in order with nothing since strong enough to shuffle them.

Twelve places in the ocean are laying down the same sediment today, and someone has measured how fast at each. Twelve numbers, resampled the same way you just resampled the films, each one implying how long 100 m would take.
