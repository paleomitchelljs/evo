      3 · Three lineages

      Joe Thornton, an excellent first baseman, specializes in reconstructing ancient proteins & exploring how individual mutations that accumulated over time changed their function. This example is a simplified version of one of his types of analyses.

      An ancestral sequence (CAGGTTACA) diversifies into three competing lineages in a population. Lineage A mutates to GAGGTTACA, Lineage B mutates to TAGGTTACA, and Lineage C mutates to AAGGTTACA. Each lineage then acquires the exact same mutations in the exact same order.

      Despite all later mutations being the same, because that first base pair remains different (A/T/G), the entire structure of the three proteins is different. Each has a different biochemical activity (y-axis). For this question, assume receptor sensitivity is positively correlated with fitness.

      The colored lines below show the “full” potential sensitivity curve for each trajectory across 8 subsequent substitutions. The solid black dots indicate the highest sensitivity each of these three allele trajectories can reach.

      Imagine 10 populations that all start at the same allele & all start with the 3 of the 1-mutation alleles shown above.
