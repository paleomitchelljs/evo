# Structure and philosophy

These lessons form the practice students will get to understand the core logic of the evolutionary process: how different forces shape the frequency of transmissible traits.

A conceptualization of evolution as (1) differential reproduction of (2) units with (3) transmissible characteristics using the forces of Mark Vellend's "evolutionary soundboard".

These lessons are meant to build on each other when taken in sequence. And to foreshadow one another where possible. Shared assets and interactives that can be built upon when reused in later lessons are a primary design focus.

Causal inference is key, and detailed regression modeling is the primary tool. The difference of stochastic and deterministic parts, and how models are built, fit, and evaluated is a throughline.

The goal is to take students who have no math or statistical background, and have only had introductory level biology courses, and build in them a deep enough intuition about statistics, graph interpretation, and evolution that they can meaningfully interpret and reason about Price's equation and the ideas of species, hierarchical selection, and individuality among other topics.

No vocabulary should be taught in the lessons, and jargon should be avoided where possible. Where not possible, terms should be restricted only to biological jargon familiar to an introductory student (e.g., meiosis, allele, gene, locus).

Activities should strive to build intuition and enforce interpretation. Activities should require interaction and interpretation of the graph/displays and ideally direct manipulation where possible. Embracing uncertainty and determining causal effects are major goals.

Text should be extremely light, with a focus on discovery through engagement with the data, models, and diagrams.
